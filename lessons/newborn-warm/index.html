Keeping the Newborn Warm

A newborn loses heat far faster than an adult and can't regulate their own temperature — a baby is hypothermic once body temperature drops below 36.5°C, which raises the risk of illness and death. This short lesson walks through the WHO Warm Chain — ten simple, linked steps that protect every baby from becoming dangerously cold or overheated in the first hours and days of life.

⏱ 18–22 min 🎯 Health workers & birth attendants 📋 8 short sections, checks along the way

By the end, you'll be able to

  • ✓ Apply each of the 10 Warm Chain steps during and after birth
  • ✓ Recognise the danger signs of a too-cold or too-hot baby and know when to escalate

Key point

Newborn hypothermia is usually caused by a gap in knowledge or routine — not a lack of equipment. That's exactly why every step in this lesson matters, in every setting.

Quick check

Which of these is not one of the 10 steps of the Warm Chain?

Correct: bathing immediately is the opposite of Step 5, which is to postpone bathing.

Section 2 · Steps 1 & 2

Set the stage, then dry immediately

Step 1 — Warm delivery room. Have the room at 25°C or warmer, with no draughts, before the baby arrives. Don't judge the room's temperature by how comfortable it feels to you as an adult — it needs to be warm enough for a baby.

Step 2 — Dry immediately. The moment the baby is born, dry them thoroughly with a clean, warm cloth — including the head. Then remove that wet cloth and replace it with a dry one. A wet baby loses heat through evaporation extremely quickly, even in a warm room.

Key point

Have your warm, dry cloths ready and within reach before every birth — this step only works if there's no delay.

Quick check

What's the minimum recommended temperature for the delivery room?

Correct: 25°C or warmer, with no draughts — judged for the baby, not for adult comfort.

Section 3 · Steps 3 & 4

Skin-to-skin, then breastfeed

Step 3 — Skin-to-skin contact (also known as Kangaroo Mother Care, or KMC). Place the dried baby directly on the mother's bare chest or abdomen, then cover both mother and baby together with a warm dry cloth. This keeps the baby at exactly the right temperature — the mother's body acts as a natural, self-adjusting heat source.

Step 4 — Breastfeed within the hour. Skin-to-skin contact also gives the baby the best chance to start breastfeeding early, ideally within the first hour of life, which gives the baby energy and supports their immune system.

Key point

Skin-to-skin isn't just comfort — treat it as active thermal treatment. Avoid separating mother and baby for routine weighing, washing, or paperwork during this window unless there's a medical reason.

Quick check

What's the best way to keep a healthy newborn at the right temperature right after birth?

Correct: skin-to-skin contact — the mother's body naturally keeps the baby at the right temperature and supports early feeding.

Section 4 · Step 5

Postpone the bath

Step 5 — Postpone the bath. Right after birth, only wipe off visible blood or meconium — don't give a full bath. Wait at least 24 hours before the baby's first bath, and longer if the baby is preterm, very small, unwell, or the room is cold.

When it is time to bathe: make sure the room is warm, test the water against the back of your hand or your wrist, wash and rinse the baby fairly quickly, then dry them thoroughly — including their hair — put a hat on, and return them to skin-to-skin contact straight away.

Before weighing a baby at any point, wrap them snugly first so they don't get cold on the scale.

Watch for

An early bath is one of the most common preventable causes of a chilled newborn — it can undo everything the earlier steps achieved.

Quick check

How long should you wait before a healthy baby's first bath?

Correct: at least 24 hours — longer if the baby is small, preterm, unwell, or the room is cold.

Section 5 · Step 6 & monitoring

Dress for warmth, then check often

Step 6 — Dress the baby in light, loose layers — generally one or two more layers than an adult would wear comfortably in the same room. A hat matters more than people expect, since a newborn loses a lot of heat through the head. A light blanket on top is fine, but avoid over-wrapping.

A baby can become dangerously hot just as easily as dangerously cold — and can't tell you when either is happening. At facility level, check and record actual temperature with a thermometer at routine intervals. Where a thermometer isn't available, use the back of your hand on the baby's feet and belly as a fallback check.

  • Cold feet and belly — warm urgently: skin-to-skin contact with extra blankets over both mother and baby, then recheck.
  • Warm feet and warm belly — a good sign, keep going as normal.
  • Flushed skin, sweating, or fast breathing — signs of overheating: remove a layer and recheck.

Escalate to a senior clinician or higher-level facility if the baby

  • Stays too cold or too hot after adjusting, or doesn't seem well
  • Feeds poorly or seems unusually sleepy/floppy
  • Breathes fast or with difficulty

Quick check

A baby's feet and belly feel cold to the touch. What should you do first?

Correct: warm urgently with skin-to-skin under extra blankets, then check again — cold feet and belly together are a warning sign.

Section 6 · Steps 7, 8, 9 & 10

Keep the chain going

Step 7 — Mother and baby together. Room the baby with the mother rather than a separate nursery, so warmth, feeding, and monitoring continue without interruption.

Step 8 — Warm transport. If a baby must travel to another facility, keep them skin-to-skin where possible, or use a warmed transport method, and check their temperature before and after the journey.

Step 9 — Warm resuscitation. Resuscitation itself — airway, breathing, stimulation — is covered in a separate module. The one thing to carry from this lesson: it should always happen under a heat source, never on a cold surface away from warmth. Warmth supports resuscitation; it doesn't replace it.

Step 10 — Training and awareness. Every health worker and caregiver needs to know these steps. This lesson is itself a link in the Warm Chain.

Quick check

Back in the Warm Chain overview, the chain used two colours — teal for "every birth, every time" and navy for "situational." Which of these steps is situational?

Correct: warm transport only applies when a baby needs to move to another facility — most steps apply to every birth, every time.

The Warm Chain, at a glance

  1. Warm room — 25°C+, no draughts
  2. Dry immediately — then swap for a dry cloth
  3. Skin-to-skin — straight onto mother's chest
  4. Breastfeed — within the first hour
  5. Delay the bath — at least 24 hours
  6. Dress in layers — plus a hat, not too many
  7. Keep mother & baby together
  8. Warm transport — if the baby must travel
  9. Warm resuscitation — under a heat source; full steps in the resuscitation module
  10. Train & remind — every worker, every time
